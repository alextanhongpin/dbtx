How to interpret results

Cumulative reward
Total successes recorded so far. Higher is better for the same reward definition and decision horizon.
Running reward rate
Successes divided by completed observations. Pending feedback is excluded. On live curves, late feedback is attributed to its original decision; these are current outcomes grouped by selection order, not historical knowledge at each step.
Allocation
The fraction of decisions assigned to each arm. Early spread indicates exploration; later concentration shows a policy's preference.
Cumulative pseudo-regret
In simulations, the sum of the best true reward probability minus each selected arm's true probability. Lower is better. Live runs cannot calculate it.
Uncertainty and feedback
Few observations leave wide posterior intervals. Missing or selectively reported feedback can distort estimates. Pending decisions never become failures automatically and may cause repeated selection of unobserved arms.

Worked example

If the best simulated arm succeeds with probability 0.6 and a selected arm has probability 0.3, that decision adds 0.3 pseudo-regret, whether its observed reward is success or failure. An arm with 8 successes from 10 observed decisions has an 80% observed success rate and a Beta(9,3) posterior mean of 75%. If it also has 5 pending decisions, those do not enter either estimate.

Adaptive allocation does not automatically establish causal uplift. Compare policies across repeated matched simulations; do not interpret a single high reward curve or narrow model-based interval as a statistically proven winner. V1 assumes stationary, independent binary rewards.
